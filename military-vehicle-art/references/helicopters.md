# Helicopters

Lock the referenced configuration: main/tail rotor, coaxial, tandem, or another specified arrangement. Refine fuselage/cockpit, tail structure, rotor hubs and blades, landing gear/skids, and equipment. Do not attach a generic tail rotor to every design.

Check fuselage/rotor ratios, rotor-hub placement, tail alignment, glazing, and visible attachment supports. Use intended blade pitch/twist appearance from references; avoid flat rectangular paddles as a final close-up blade. Test rotor-disc and blade clearances in representative poses. Keep a clean editable static blade source; use an appropriate motion-blur representation for presentation rather than random transparent planes.

Separate pivot setup from an actual animation test. Check rotor axes, landing supports, tail assembly, and other neighbours after geometry changes. Inspect neutral clay views to expose cockpit and fuselage curvature; check glass materials without using bright opaque placeholder rectangles. A blurry spinning rotor can hide construction problems: inspect the static source too.

If improving materials on an approved rigged asset, preserve geometry, names, hierarchy, and bindings unless authorized. Isolate lighting/material defects before replacing the model.
